Wedding-day emergency kit: small fixes, less scrambling

A thoughtful emergency kit is less about buying every bridal gadget and more about solving the few problems that can interrupt a wedding day. Organize one compact bag, assign it to a trusted person, and keep it somewhere accessible.

How this guide was made: An editorial planning checklist based on practical selection criteria, not a claim that we personally tested or purchased the products mentioned. Examples and recommendations are not paid placements. Last reviewed November 23, 2026.

1. Clothing, fabric and footwear

Start with the outfit and location, because delicate fabrics, fasteners and shoe choices change what belongs in the bag. A compact sewing kit, neutral safety pins, fashion tape and a lint roller can help with minor issues—but test any product on an inconspicuous area when possible.

For satin, silk, embellished fabric or rental garments, follow the garment's care instructions. An inappropriate stain remover can make a small mark worse.

  • Sewing kit with thread matched to the outfit; a few safety pins.
  • Double-sided fashion tape if suitable for the garment and skin.
  • Lint roller; microfiber cloth; spare hosiery where relevant.
  • Comfortable spare shoes and blister protection.

2. Personal comfort and hygiene

Plan for the practical realities of a long day: changing temperatures, standing for photographs, and meals that run late. Pack only items the couple and wedding party will actually use.

Do not put loose medications in an unlabelled communal kit or offer someone medication without checking their own needs, allergies and instructions.

  • Tissues, hand wipes, sanitary products, small mirror.
  • Water, easy-to-carry snacks, breath mints where allowed.
  • Bandages and blister cushions in original packaging.
  • Hair ties, bobby pins and a travel comb.

3. Power and communication

A phone with the wedding timeline and vendor contacts is often more useful than a bag of rarely used gadgets. Assign one person to handle vendor questions so the couple is not fielding calls.

Check whether the venue has reliable cell service and easy access to outlets; bring the right cables rather than assuming everyone uses the same connector.

  • Charged portable power bank and compatible charging cables.
  • Printed day-of timeline and vendor contact sheet.
  • Contact numbers for venue coordinator, transportation and key family.
  • A small envelope for receipts, tips or last-minute notes.

4. Assemble the kit around your venue

Outdoor ceremonies may call for weather-appropriate comfort items; a ballroom reception often benefits more from shoe and wardrobe backups. Venue rules may restrict sprays, candles, confetti or outside food.

Keep sharp items and potentially irritating products safely contained, especially if children will be nearby. Ask your venue whether it supplies basic first aid before duplicating equipment.

5. Delegate and label

Pack each category in a separate pouch. Label the kit, keep a simple inventory card, and tell one person where it will be. The goal is to prevent a quick fix from turning into a scavenger hunt.

Two days before the wedding, check batteries, expiration dates and the completeness of the contact list. If the wedding party is getting ready at a different address, consider which supplies need to travel with them.

Pair the kit with a planning system

The CedarVibes Wedding Planning Command Center includes wedding organization tools for budgets, guests, vendors and the day-of timeline. Use the kit checklist alongside those planning records.

  • Choose a small, easy-to-carry bag rather than a large, heavy box.
  • Assign a kit owner and tell the day-of coordinator where it will be.
  • Review garment care requirements before buying repair products.
  • Save the venue and vendor contacts offline.
